# Sales Value Negotiation

Negotiate the whole package against realistic alternatives. Scope, delivery, payment timing, support, and risk may matter differently to each side. Use for considered agreements, not every fixed-price transaction.

## Diagnose before conceding

| Buyer signal | Test | Useful response |
|---|---|---|
| “Too expensive” | Affordability, expected value, comparison, or payment timing? | Address the specific constraint |
| “Competitor is cheaper” | Equivalent scope, service, term, implementation, and risk? | Compare like-for-like evidence |
| “We need a guarantee” | Which uncertain outcome, measured how, under whose control? | Clarify risk and feasible proof |
| “Discount and we sign” | Remaining approvals and authorized decision-maker? | Verify conditions before trading |

## Prepare defensible packages

1. Separate stated interests from hypotheses. Use buyer-validated outcomes and relevant objective benchmarks; never invent ROI, competing offers, or deadlines.
2. Identify feasible no-agreement alternatives and their costs. Select the best alternative (BATNA), set a target and minimum acceptable package, and confirm concession authority. A hoped-for deal is not an available alternative.
3. Price delivery effort, payment timing, support load, implementation risk, and opportunity cost. Keep private limits out of buyer-facing drafts.
4. Construct two or three genuinely acceptable packages with approximately equivalent seller value but different buyer tradeoffs. Present complete alternatives; ask which fits better and why; revise accordingly. Equivalence requires a cost/risk model, not equal sticker prices.
5. Make concessions explicit and, where appropriate, conditional on reciprocal changes. Recalculate the entire revised package against the minimum; escalate anything outside authority. Record tentative versus approved terms.

Illustrative arithmetic: a $30,000 project with $10,000 delivery cost and a reduced-scope $27,000 project with $7,000 cost contribute $20,000 each before overhead. They are not equivalent if payment timing, liability, or delivery risk differs. Do not promise identical outcomes without evidence.

## Pitfalls

- Bundling hides economics if the buyer cherry-picks each package's cheapest term; reprice the combination.
- Giving concessions before diagnosing the obstacle can sacrifice margin without resolving it.
- Artificial scarcity, sham alternatives, and pressure destroy informed agreement.
- A prepared proposal does not authorize sending, accepting terms, or binding either party.
