# Sales Discovery and Qualification

Buyer enthusiasm is not buying evidence. Discovery tests whether a consequential problem and a credible path to change exist; qualification allocates further effort. Use for considered B2B purchases; avoid interrogating transactional buyers.

## Evidence before stage labels

Record each claim with its speaker, date, supporting artifact, and status: confirmed, inferred, unknown, or contradicted. A populated CRM field is not confirmation.

| Question | Evidence to seek | Missing evidence means |
|---|---|---|
| Why change? | Recent incident, affected workflow, consequence, baseline | Test the problem before pitching |
| Why this solution? | Buyer-owned success criteria and proof required | Design a relevant evaluation |
| Who can decide? | Funding authority, affected users, approval steps | Validate the buying route |
| Why now? | Buyer deadline and cost of delay versus competing priorities | Keep timing uncertain |

## Run an evidence-seeking conversation

1. Research public context; mark account-specific assumptions. Test assumptions rather than inventing prospect facts.
2. Use SPIN question types flexibly: confirm necessary situation details, examine a concrete problem, explore consequences, then ask what a useful improvement would enable. Follow answers instead of mechanically completing a script.
3. Quantify with the buyer: frequency, affected volume, time or cost per incident, and measurement period. Label estimates and owners. Separate recoverable cash, released capacity, and speculative revenue; do not add overlapping benefits.
4. Check decision criteria, authority, evaluation steps, procurement constraints, and alternatives, including keeping the current approach. Unknown budget or authority calls for investigation, not an invented qualification score.
5. Summarize evidence and contradictions. Recommend advance, investigate, defer, or decline. Advancement needs a useful buyer-agreed action, owner, and date; deferral needs a reopening condition.

Example: “Reporting takes too long” supports investigation. A verified weekly delay, affected decision, and baseline review support evaluation. Neither proves budget approval or a purchase commitment.

## Pitfalls

- Amplifying hypothetical pain creates pressure, not evidence; accept that doing nothing may be rational.
- Treating one person's estimate as organizational agreement inflates the business case.
- Counting meetings or demos as progress hides unchanged decision risk.
- Completing an acronym is not a calibrated win probability or permission to update records or contact buyers.
