## Original scenario: scope changes before evidence gets old

All companies, figures, events, and decisions below are hypothetical. Proposed action is an internal recommendation pending the user's authority to engage or change systems.

| Step | Known evidence | Naive account-level baseline | Scoped decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Operations owner approves $20,000 sandbox pilot for one unit; tested function passes | Record account as qualified | Confirm pilot-only funding and tested function; production rights unknown |
| 2 | Next day, requested rollout becomes $200,000 across two units | Carry approval forward because it is recent | Revalidate authority for amount/unit scope, production criteria, and review route; preserve observed sandbox result |
| 3 | Former advocate changes role; successor is friendly | Transfer champion label to successor | Influence/advocacy unresolved until supported; friendliness is insufficient |
| 4 | Buyer confirms a finance committee must approve rollout; security review has not begun | Keep earlier forecast date | Map both prerequisites with their owners; investigate timing rather than infer a close date |
| 5 | Committee approves stated rollout scope, and security owner supplies applicable clearance | Erase previous gaps | Preserve prior entries and superseding evidence; only resolved claims become confirmed; next action still buyer-agreed |

The remedy removes unsupported carryover; it does not establish that the rollout will close. A pilot can demonstrate a function while leaving production readiness and funding unresolved. If deployment architecture changes, even the relevance of that functional result needs examination.

## Counterexamples and applicability limits

- A new stakeholder does not automatically negate all tests, invalidate signed history, or establish a new veto. Determine their actual role and affected scope.
- A supplier spelling correction or new CRM edit timestamp alone is not a substantive buying-decision change.
- An approval can have an explicit expiration or conditions. Those control its validity; do not replace them with an invented 30-day rule.
- No known change does not prove continued validity. Review at buyer-agreed checkpoints and inspect explicit conditions; distinguish unobserved from contradicted.
- A superseding authority explicitly rejects the rollout: that claim is contradicted, not merely awaiting revalidation. Prior pilot approval may remain historical fact.
- Expansion does not always require a more senior individual; current delegation limits and governance decide. Do not automatically pursue the CEO or bypass the existing relationship.

## Validation performed

A structured review traced the five-step scenario and six counterexamples against the matrix. It checked that scope change reopens affected claims, unrelated evidence is preserved, silence is not rejection, explicit rejection is distinguished, and explicit approval conditions take precedence.
