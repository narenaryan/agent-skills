# Marketing Positioning Tests

Buyer alternatives determine differentiators; differentiators enable value; value identifies best-fit buyers; category supplies the comparison frame. Copy expresses this dependency chain.

## Evidence before wording

Build a claim ledger from interviews, lost deals, and product evidence. Separate facts from assumptions; record source, date, customer context, and counterevidence. Missing evidence requires research, not invented quotes.

| Decision | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Alternative | What buyers actually use or shortlist, including manual work |
| Differentiator | Capability demonstrably absent or weaker in that alternative |
| Value | Customer outcome enabled by that capability |
| Best-fit segment | Observable circumstances making that outcome important |
| Category | Familiar frame whose expectations the product can satisfy |

Feature comparisons may not resolve migration costs or uncertainty about making the wrong purchase.

## Testable recipe

1. Draft two genuinely different positioning hypotheses. Keep price, offer, and visuals stable when testing wording.
2. Recruit relevant buyers by problem and purchase context. Show each version without explanation; counterbalance presentation order when people see both.
3. Ask buyers to paraphrase the product, intended buyer, outcome, and difference. Ask which claim needs proof and what prevents consideration, not which slogan they like.
4. Record comprehension errors, expectations, relevance, and credibility separately. Preserve disconfirming responses rather than creating one preference score.
5. Revise the weakest link. Treat qualitative results as diagnostic; validate any claimed conversion lift with an appropriately designed experiment and downstream quality metric.

Example hypothesis: “Release approvals for distributed engineering teams” needs evidence that cross-time-zone handoffs matter and that asynchronous approval reduces them. “Save ten hours weekly” remains unusable until substantiated.

## Sources and scope

- [Dunford: positioning components](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning): practitioner framework, especially B2B; not causal proof of demand.
- [CXL: clarity testing](https://cxl.com/blog/improving-clarity/): comprehension diagnostics; short exposure cannot establish purchase behavior.

## Pitfalls

- Competitors found online may never enter buyers' consideration sets.
- A new category adds an education burden; novelty alone is not differentiation.
- Friendly existing customers can hide acquisition-stage confusion.
- Preference scores do not establish lift, and stronger wording cannot repair unsupported claims.
